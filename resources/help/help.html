PhotoLife Help

PhotoLife builds a catalogue alongside your nature-photo library and hangs each photo on the tree of life. It never moves, renames or deletes the photos in your library — it reads them and keeps its own database. (The one exception is Organise New Photos, which moves a new download folder's photos into taxon folders — only when you commit, never deleting anything, and it can be undone.) The only times it writes to a photo file are when you explicitly ask it to (see What PhotoLife writes to your photos).

The workflow at a glance

New to PhotoLife? The Getting Started guide takes you through these steps one at a time, with a picture of each and a button that opens the right part of PhotoLife; each step ticks itself off as you finish it. It opens by itself until you have a photo library and a reference tree, and any time from File or Help → Getting Started… (or the button on the empty library screen). Click a picture in it to see it larger.

  1. Add a folder of photos to the library → PhotoLife scans it.
  2. Build a reference tree for a group and region (e.g. Orchids of Victoria) from iNaturalist.
  3. Import a checklist CSV to add rare species and conservation status (optional).
  4. Match Library → every photo is tied to a taxon by its folder and file name.
  5. Review the ones PhotoLife was unsure about.
  6. Browse the tree and watch your coverage grow.

Steps 2–4 need an internet connection (they query the iNaturalist API); everything is cached, so afterwards PhotoLife works offline. If you post to iNaturalist, the iNaturalist tools can also download your own observations into the library and compare your iNaturalist list with your trees.

Finding your way around

The View menu has five mutually-exclusive modes (the first four are also on the toolbar):

  • Reference Tree — the tab row underneath shows Photos of Tree Selection, Map, My Best Shots, Unphotographed Taxa, Photographic Life List, Not in Any Tree and Reference Photos — all of them scoped to whatever taxon or reference tree you have selected on the left.
  • All Library Photos — every photo in the catalogue, regardless of any reference tree. PhotoLife starts in this mode. This is where the Show status filter in the toolbar applies.
  • Review Unmatched — the review queue (see below). Its count badge shows how many photos are waiting.
  • iNaturalist — tools that work with an iNaturalist account (see iNaturalist tools). Also reachable from the iNaturalist Tools menu.
  • Organise New Photos — sorting a new download folder into taxon folders (see Organise new photos). Also reachable from the Organise menu.

The Reference Trees panel on the left (tree picker, search box, tree, coverage summary) stays visible no matter which mode you're in, so you can still search or select a taxon while reviewing or browsing the whole library.

Organise new photos

After a day out, copy the day's photos into a folder (say Lakeside 2025-10-18), then choose Organise → Open Download Folder… (or View → Organise New Photos). The photos appear as a filmstrip in the order they were taken — RAW and JPEG of a shot together — with the selected photo large below it, and above that how far through the folder you are (Photo 12 of 340). The mouse wheel scrolls the strip; Grid shows them all at once (F11, or View → Full Screen, for full screen, anywhere in PhotoLife).

Import from Card… does the copying for you. Put the camera's card in (a drive with a DCIM folder is found by itself) or choose any folder of photos, choose where the photos go, and optionally a name. It lists what's on the card by the day each photo was taken, then copies them into a folder per day — 2026-11-13, or 2026-11-13 Anakie with a name — and opens the first in Organise. Each copy is read back and compared with the original before it counts, and keeps the photo's date. Nothing on the card is changed or deleted, so format it in the camera once you're happy. Photos imported before (from any card) are recognised and skipped, even after you've sorted them away; tick the box to copy them again. An import that's stopped, or a card pulled out, carries on where it stopped next time.

A big folder opens a page of 250 photos at a time, so you can start straight away: the next 250 are read as you scroll towards the end of those shown (the counts say how many are loaded so far). When you carry on with a folder, enough is read to reach the last photo you named. Organise into Folders reads any photos not yet loaded first, since the last name carries on into them.

Photos with a GPS position embedded are marked GEO. Turn on Places to show where each was taken — the town or locality, under the date — which can help tell similar species apart. Places are looked up on OpenStreetMap only for the photos in view, about one a second, so they appear as you scroll (places already known from your library's photos appear at once, even offline).

  • Click a photo to focus it and see a larger preview. Click the first photo of each taxon, then press N (or right-click and choose Name This Photo) to enter its name — suggestions come from your trees' common and scientific names (↓ to choose one), or anything typed is used as it is. Every photo after it, up to the next named one, goes with that name; a coloured band shows each run. To use a name you've already given in this folder (back to a species seen earlier in the day), right-click the photo and choose it under Name As — most recent first, and its number key picks it while the menu is open.
  • With a photo focused, press B to mark it as a best shot (a gold star): when you organise, it's rated 5 stars — in the JPEG, or in an .xmp sidecar beside a RAW, which is never written to — so when you add it to the library it arrives as a Best Shot of its species.
  • Not sure what it is? Press I (or right-click → Identify with iNaturalist…, or Identify in the Name box) and iNaturalist's computer vision suggests what the photo shows; click one to name the photo with it. Identify Runs… does a whole folder: it guesses where each new taxon starts (more than 30 seconds between shots, or what you set), asks about the first photo of each, and lists them with the best suggestion chosen for you to check, change or leave out. Photos you've already named are left alone. See Identify with iNaturalist.
  • Photos without a GEO mark can be given a location: select them and press G (or right-click → Set Location on Map…), then put a pin where they were taken — click the map, search for a place, or paste coordinates such as -25.954, 153.105. Other photos in the folder taken within a few hours that have a location show as grey dots, and the map opens on the one nearest in time: double-click a dot to use it. The place name under the map is the one renaming will use. It's written straight away (see below), so the GEO mark and place appear at once; use it on a geotagged photo to move its location. With several photos selected, the arrows under the photo step through them and the pin shows where each one already is (no pin when it has no location yet), until you place the pin yourself; only a pin you placed is written. Undo Organise doesn't remove a location.
  • To take a location off again, select the photos and right-click → Remove Location…. After you confirm, the GPS position is taken out of the JPEG and out of a RAW's .xmp sidecar (the sidecar stays, with anything else it holds), and the GEO mark goes. A location held inside a RAW file itself is removed only when Also write it into the RAW files is on in Set Location on Map; otherwise you're told which files still have one. It can't be undone, so a position the camera or phone recorded is gone for good.
  • Bursts and choosing the sharpest. Photos taken within 2 seconds of each other are a burst: a bar joins them along the foot of their cells, and once PhotoLife has looked at them (in the background) the sharpest is marked SHARPEST. That's judged from fine detail in the middle of the frame, so treat it as a hint — a sharp background behind a soft bird can fool it. Press C (or right-click → Compare Side by Side) to see two to four photos at once — the ones selected, or the burst of the one you're on — zoomed and panned together: the wheel zooms them round the same spot, A or a double-click shows them 1:1, and R, B and N reject, star or name the one under the pointer. Right-click → Keep This, Reject the Rest of the Burst does the culling in one go. Nothing is rejected without you.
  • Not sure? Press U on a named photo to mark its name as uncertain: the run shows (not sure), the counts say how many, and Shift+U takes you to the next one to check. When you organise, you're asked whether such runs go into cf. folders — Pterostylis cf. nana, cf. Scarlet Robin — apart from ones you're sure of.
  • Notes for a run. Press T (or right-click → Notes for This Run…) to note the sex, life stage, alive or dead, flowers or fruit, and anything else, for every photo of the run. They're written into the photos when you organise (a JPEG's own XMP, and the .xmp sidecar beside a RAW; the RAW isn't changed), so other programs show them as the description, and they're offered as annotations when you upload to iNaturalist.
  • Geotag on arrival. When a folder opens and some of its photos without a location were taken while a GPS track was recording, a line above the photos says so: Geotag them from it places them all (as below), and Not now hides it for the session. The track can be a .gpx file in the folder, or one in your Tracks folder (set in GPX Track…): point it at the folder your phone's tracking app saves to, and the track that covers the most photos is used.
  • From a GPS track. If you recorded a track while you were out (a .gpx file from a phone app or a GPS unit), GPX Track… above the photos chooses it for the folder — a single .gpx file in the folder is picked up by itself, and the button is marked while a track is loaded. Then select photos and right-click → Create Geotag from GPX (or Shift+G): each one gets the position the track recorded at the time it was taken, worked out between the two track points either side of it, and written straight away as Set Location on Map writes it. A photo taken more than 5 minutes from any track point is left alone, and the message says how many were. If some already have a location you're asked whether to replace it. The track file itself is only read, never changed or moved.
    A track's times are exact; a camera's are whatever its clock said. PhotoLife reads photo times as this computer's time zone, so if the camera was set to another zone, or its clock was off, set the Camera clock correction in GPX Track… (the line under it counts how many photos then fall on the track). The correction is remembered for the folder.
    Set Location on Map… draws the track as a line, with a ring where the photo's time falls on it: double-click the ring to use that spot, or click anywhere along the line (hover over it to see the time) to choose another.
  • With a photo focused, press R to mark it as a reject, or V (or double-click it) to view it full size. Right-click for these actions, rename or clear a name, and other options. Delete still marks the selected photos as rejects; the ⤢ corner also opens a photo full size. In the full-size view, A shows it at actual pixels (1:1) to check sharpness — drag to pan — and the arrow keys keep that zoom and spot from photo to photo (see The image viewer). R there marks the photo as a reject and moves on to the next, so you can cull a burst full size; on a rejected photo it takes the reject back. The Reject button beside the arrows does the same. N there (or the Name button) names the photo on view, and the photos after it up to the next named one, just as in the grid: the Name popup opens over the photo, and the caption shows the name.
  • To stop a name carrying forward, focus the last photo of that taxon — not the first photo of the next one — and press E, or right-click and choose Mark as Last Photo of “name” (the menu shows the taxon's name). That photo keeps the name and shows last of its taxon; the photos after it are left unnamed until you name the next taxon. The mark is saved with your progress; Not the Last Photo of “name” (or E again) removes it.
  • Your names and rejects are kept in the folder as you go, so you can stop and carry on later where you left off. To come back after visiting another view, choose Organise → Continue Organising “folder” (Ctrl+Shift+O) — it returns to the folder as you left it, or, after restarting PhotoLife, reopens the last download folder at the last photo you named. (View → Organise New Photos also switches back.)
  • Organise into Folders… shows what will happen, then moves each run into a folder of its name inside the download folder (Lakeside 2025-10-18/Flame Robin/DSC_1234.NEF), and rejects into Rejects. File names are kept; RAW, JPEG and sidecar files (.xmp …) move together; two runs given the same name share a folder; nothing is overwritten (a name already taken gets “(2)”) or deleted. Photos before the first name stay where they are.
    Tick Also rename the photos to name each one for its folder, where it was taken and the date — Scarlet Robin - Lakeside, Victoria, 2025-10-18.NEF (just the date when it has no GPS). Later photos with the same name get “(2)”, “(3)”… in the order they were taken; a photo's RAW, JPEG and sidecars share its name. Places not already known are looked up on OpenStreetMap first, about one a second. Rejects keep their names.
  • Undo Organise… takes back a whole Organise into Folders — every folder it made, not just one taxon. It first lists exactly what will go back (when that organise was done, each folder and how many photos), then returns the photos to the download folder under their original file names, with their names and rejects, and removes the emptied folders. Organised more than once? Each Undo takes back the most recent one, so you can step back through them.

Move the organised folder into your library as usual — or organise it where it already is inside the library, and PhotoLife catalogues the new folders straight away; named after their taxa, they match automatically.

1. Add your photo folders

Library → Add Folder to Library… and choose a folder. PhotoLife walks it, records every image, and groups a RAW file and its same-name JPEG as one capture. It skips junk (Thumbs.db, sidecar files, folders like “To Sort and Upload”).

It watches your folders afterwards and re-scans automatically when they change. Library → Rescan Library (F5) forces a full re-scan; unchanged files are never re-read, and Stop Scan cancels one in progress. A file you rename or move keeps its matches and thumbnail — PhotoLife recognises it by its contents. Rename or move one in your file manager and every grid takes the new name a moment later; open it before then and PhotoLife looks for it first, then opens it.

Library → Manage Library Folders… lists the folders being watched, flags any that have gone missing, and lets you stop watching one (its catalogued photos are kept). Library → Remove Photos With Missing Files… finds catalogued photos whose file has since vanished (moved or deleted outside PhotoLife) and lets you drop them from the catalogue.

Your folder names matter: a Family / Genus / Species tree with scientific names in the file names matches best, but PhotoLife copes with synonyms, misspellings, sp., hybrids, aggregates, and common-name folders for fauna.

Library → Import Lightroom Catalog… reads the keywords you've tagged photos with in Adobe Lightroom Classic (a .lrcat file) and uses them as an extra matching hint alongside folder and file names — handy for photos whose file names don't name the species. It needs a reference tree to exist first. Your Lightroom catalog is only read, never changed.

2. Build a reference tree

Reference Tree → New Reference Tree… Give it a name, a root taxon (Orchidaceae), an optional rank, and a region (Victoria, AU — leave blank for worldwide). PhotoLife looks up the place and taxon on iNaturalist and asks you to confirm the exact match for each before building — useful when a name is ambiguous. Once confirmed, it pulls the region’s species list and fills in the intermediate ranks so the tree is connected from kingdom down to species. A very large tree pauses every few thousand species to ask whether to keep going.

You can keep several trees and switch between them with the drop-down at the top of the Reference Trees panel on the left. From the Reference Tree menu:

  • Refresh Reference Tree re-pulls the current one from iNaturalist: species newly recorded in the place are added, and taxa you've pruned stay out. (Between refreshes, iNaturalist sometimes adds a level — a complex or a section, say Hedera helix (complex) between Hedera and the species. When a tree is shown, PhotoLife fetches any such missing level by itself, in a few seconds; until then the species sits under its nearest group the tree has.) It then checks what has gone, and lists it in Tree Changes on iNaturalist:
    • Renamed — iNaturalist now uses another name (a swap or merge). Move photos to the new name re-points the photos matched to the old one — your confirmed ones stay confirmed — and the names PhotoLife learned for it, adds the new name to the tree and prunes the old.
    • Split — now several taxa: pick which one its photos go to, or leave it for now.
    • Retired — no longer used on iNaturalist.
    • No longer recorded in the tree's place — a species with no verifiable observations there now (often a vagrant, or one whose records were re-identified). Species from an imported checklist are only listed if renamed.
    • Subspecies with no records in the tree's place — none of its observations there are named to that subspecies. Fetch Subspecies/Varieties adds every subspecies of each species, from anywhere, so a regional tree can hold many from elsewhere. But most observations are named only to species, so a subspecies can live there with no records of its own (a local bird tree may list every one): check before pruning. Prune Subspecies Without Photos sets all of those you haven't photographed to Prune at once; ones with your photos stay as they are unless you choose.
    • Empty groups — a genus or family with nothing beneath it in the tree.
    For each choose Ask again later (nothing changes), Keep (it isn't asked about again, unless it's later renamed), Prune, or Move photos to; then Apply. Photos of a pruned taxon stay in your library (under Photos not in any tree). The check needs the whole list, so it's skipped when a refresh was stopped early or picked up where an interrupted one left off.
  • Add Taxon to Reference Tree… adds a single taxon (with its ancestors) that the region's list didn't include.
  • Fetch Subspecies/Varieties… checks the tree’s species for iNaturalist subspecies, varieties and hybrids that iNat’s regular species list doesn’t report on its own. It makes a few requests per species, so it can be slow for a large tree, but it's safe to quit and re-run later.
  • Update Common Names fetches every English common name iNaturalist has for your trees’ taxa — not just its preferred one — so photos filed under another name (“Purple Swamphen” for the Australasian Swamphen) match too. The taxa your photos are matched to come first — a few minutes, with a progress window — then the library is matched again, and the rest of your trees' taxa are fetched quietly in the background, the most useful first. For big trees that takes hours (iNaturalist answers about 30 taxa every few seconds); it carries on at the next launch if you close PhotoLife, and the status bar shows how far it has got. New trees get theirs by themselves; each taxon is fetched again after 90 days.
  • Delete Reference Tree… removes a tree you no longer need (your photos and their matches are untouched).

Folders for a higher group. A photo named for a species inside a folder named for its family or order (“Formicidae/Podomyrma adelaidae …”) matches automatically — the two agree, one is just more exact. Only a photo whose name isn't within its folder's group (an orchid in an ant folder) goes to Review Unmatched.

Common names in folder and file names. A folder or photo named with a common name (“Flame Robin”) matches automatically when that name means just one species (or subspecies) in your trees; differences in hyphens, spaces and apostrophes don't matter (“Superb Fairy-wren” finds “Superb Fairywren”, “Lathams Snipe” finds “Latham's Snipe”), nor does “Robin, Flame” order. A folder can give both names — “Flame Robin · Petroica phoenicea” or “Flame Robin (Petroica phoenicea)” — and they're checked against each other. A group's common name (“Sun Orchids”), or one shared by several taxa, goes to Review Unmatched. A Common name column in an imported tree file is used the same way.

Photos still named by the camera (“DSC_1234”, “IMG_0042”, “P1010001”) take their species from the folder, so the photos in “Rosella, Crimson” become Crimson Rosella. When a photo's own name gives a different species from its folder's, the file name's species is the one suggested, and the photo waits in Review Unmatched for you to confirm it.

Right-click a taxon in the tree for more:

  • Fetch Subspecies/Varieties under “…” — the same, for just that branch.
  • Match Library Against “…” — re-checks your unresolved photos against just that group, preferring its taxa when a name is ambiguous (e.g. a common name shared with another group).
  • Show Photos of This Taxon and Open on iNaturalist.
  • Prune from Reference Tree… — removes that taxon and everything beneath it from this tree only (other trees, and your photos, are untouched). Pruned taxa stay out when the tree is refreshed; the tree is then shown as “(customized)”.

The Search taxa box under the drop-down jumps the tree to a taxon as you type — matching its scientific or common name — and press Enter to step through the other matches. If the right pane is on Photos of Tree Selection it follows along, showing that taxon’s photos. A match hidden by the “photographed only” filter un-hides it. The Ranks… button lets you hide ranks you don't care to see in the tree (e.g. collapse straight to species, skipping family/genus rows). Expand All beside it opens every branch, and keeps the tree open as you change trees or filters until you turn it off again (which folds it back to the top levels, keeping the selected taxon in view).

Columns, at the right of that row, shows the tree another way: as columns, one step down per column. Click a taxon and what's beneath it opens in the next column — from Life (or the top rank you show) down through the families and genera to the species and their subspecies. Each entry shows its scientific name with the common name under it, its rank in grey, its coverage (✔, or photographed / total), the Seen tick when the Seen column is on, and an arrow when there's more beneath it. iNaturalist doesn't give every taxon every rank, so a column can mix ranks (genera beside subfamilies, say); hide the in-between ranks with Ranks… to make the columns line up rank by rank. When a species or lower taxon is selected, a card beside the columns shows iNaturalist's photo of it, your own cover photo if you have one, its names and coverage, and Show Photos and Open on iNaturalist. Search, the filters, right-click and the photo tabs all work as they do in the tree; the panel widens for the columns, and Tree switches back (and narrows it again).

Exporting and importing a tree

Reference Tree → Export Reference Tree… saves the current tree as an Excel workbook (.xlsx) or a CSV file, with one row per taxon at every rank, in tree order. The first columns are for reading: rank, scientific and common name, the kingdom, phylum, class, order, family and genus each taxon sits in, its conservation status and origin, and whether it's recorded in the tree's region. Four more say how much of it you've photographed: Photographed (yes/no, counting photos of anything below it too), Photos, Latest photo, and for genera, families and other higher ranks Species photographed (e.g. 12 / 40) — filter on Photographed = no for a list of what's still to find. These describe your photos, not the tree, so importing ignores them. The columns after those (collapsed in the workbook; click the + above them to show them) hold what PhotoLife needs to rebuild the tree exactly: iNaturalist IDs, ancestry, and every recorded status and origin. The workbook's Tree sheet (or the # lines at the top of a CSV) holds the tree's name, root taxon, region and any groups you've pruned.

Reference Tree → Import Reference Tree… creates a new tree from such a file — to restore a backup, or to use a tree someone else exported. An exported file imports in seconds without needing the internet. You can also import a file you've made or edited yourself: only a Scientific name column (or Name, Species or Taxon) is required. Rows without an iNaturalist ID are looked up on iNaturalist, like a checklist's, and any missing higher ranks are fetched so the tree stays connected. Optional Common name, Status and Origin columns are read too.

3. Import a regional checklist

Reference Tree → Import Checklist… and pick a CSV of <name>,<conservation status> rows, optionally with whether each taxon is native or introduced (an Origin column, a third column holding values like *, introduced or native, or a * before introduced names, as VicFlora and the VBA use) (such as a state vascular-plant list). PhotoLife adds any species in the current tree’s genera — including rarities with no iNaturalist observations — and records their threat status, which then shows in the coverage panel and the tree tooltips.

A status belongs to the species, not to one tree: rows outside the current tree’s genera aren't added to it, but if PhotoLife already knows the species (from any of your trees), its status is still recorded, so every tree containing it counts it. For a broad list such as all of a state's vascular plants, import it into your broadest matching tree (e.g. Vascular Plants of Victoria) so its rarities are added too.

A checklist's statuses are recorded for the region of the tree you import it into, and each tree counts only statuses listed for its own region or a region containing it. A Victorian list therefore counts in Vascular Plants of Victoria and in a Grampians or Brisbane Ranges tree, but not in a South Australian or Australia-wide tree. PhotoLife asks iNaturalist which regions lie within which (in the background, once per region); until it knows, it compares the regions' map boundaries.

Introduced species follow the same regional rule. Besides a checklist's origin column, PhotoLife asks iNaturalist, in the background, which of each tree's taxa are introduced in the tree's region.

Australian conservation and origin data

For trees in Australia, PhotoLife also fetches, in the background and about once a month:

  • Threatened status from the Atlas of Living Australia: every state's and territory's official conservation list, and the national EPBC Act list. Each counts only in trees within its region — Victoria's list in Victorian trees, the EPBC list in every Australian tree.
  • Native or introduced, state by state, for plants from the Australian Plant Census (a download of about 100 MB, kept on disk). A plant naturalised in a state counts as introduced there; one native there (even if also naturalised in parts of it) counts as native.

Only species already in your trees are matched, so after building or importing a tree PhotoLife re-matches automatically, using the lists it has already downloaded (both are kept on disk and shared by all your catalogues).Reference Tree → Update Threatened & Introduced Status (Australia) forces a fresh download of both, showing a progress window (list by list for the ALA, megabytes for the APC) that you can cancel; anything already saved is kept.

When several records apply to a species, the most specific region wins (a state's listing over the EPBC list), then the more authoritative source: for threatened status, the ALA's lists over an imported checklist (a checklist still supplies categories the ALA doesn't have, such as Victoria's advisory “Rare”); for native/introduced, the Australian Plant Census, then a checklist, then iNaturalist. The APC has no nation-wide flag, so an Australia-wide tree's introduced counts come from iNaturalist.

4. Match your photos

Matching → Match Library. PhotoLife first rescans the library, so files and folders you've just renamed or moved are matched under their new names (this normally takes a second or so, since unchanged files aren't re-read). Then, for each capture, it parses the folder path and the file name (plus any Lightroom keywords), reconciles them against the cached taxonomy (including synonyms), scores the result, and either applies it automatically or puts it in the review queue. A progress window shows both steps — the scan, then each photo being matched — for the whole library and for Match Library Against “…” on a single taxon or group alike. Cancel stops whichever step is running; matches already made are kept, and if the scan is stopped the match is skipped.

Automatic matches simply follow a renamed file to its new name. A decision you made yourself is never changed without asking: if you rename a photo you've identified, and its new name points to a different taxon, Match Library asks rather than guessing. After the run, a Renamed Photos window lists each such photo with what you identified it as and what its name now says. Checked photos are re-associated with the taxon in their new name. Unchecked photos keep your identification and aren't asked about again unless renamed once more. Decide Later changes nothing. The first run after updating also lists older identifications whose file names clearly contradict them.

In the All Library Photos view a coloured dot on each thumbnail shows its status: ● automatic, ● needs review, ● unmatched, ● confirmed by you. The Show box in the toolbar filters by status, and View → Display… chooses which fields (name, matched taxon, date, filename, locality, file type) appear under each thumbnail in the photo grids.

Right-click a photo (here or in any other grid) to open it in your file manager, star or unstar it as a best shot, remove it from the catalogue, Attempt to Match with iNat Observation to Fetch Data, or Set Location on Map… to give the selected photos a location or move theirs, as in Organise (see below).

5. Review the uncertain matches

Switch to the Review Unmatched view. The list on the left shows everything PhotoLife was unsure about, grouped by matching name; Collapse all/Expand all and the Filter photos box (by name, folder or guess) help you work one genus at a time. For the selected photo you see it side-by-side with an iNaturalist reference photo of the highlighted candidate, the engine’s guess, a ranked list of alternatives, and a taxon search box. Identify (iNaturalist) (Ctrl+Shift+I) asks iNaturalist's computer vision about the photo; a suggestion you click goes to the top of the list, ready to confirm (see Identify with iNaturalist). Then:

  • Confirm — accept the highlighted taxon. PhotoLife learns the name, so the same oddity is never queued again.
  • Reject — there is no good match; drop it from the queue.
  • Genus only — tie it to the genus (for sp. photos).
  • Not a taxon — this folder is a place or a staging folder, not a species.
  • Skip — come back to it later.
  • Undo — reverses your last Confirm/Reject/Genus only/Not a taxon/“Use Selected Taxon on Tree” decision, putting that photo straight back in the queue. Only the last single-photo decision can be undone.
  • Use Selected Taxon on Tree — confirms the photo as whatever taxon is currently selected in the Reference Trees panel on the left, so you can browse the tree with one hand and confirm matches with the other.
  • Apply to folder — gives the chosen taxon to every remaining pending photo in the same folder. Tick Include subfolders first to extend that to the whole subtree. Always asks you to confirm how many photos will change before doing it.
  • Ignore Folder… — marks a folder (and its subfolders) as not containing taxa at all — a place name or a staging/miscellany folder — and drops every pending photo in it from the queue in one go.
  • Attempt to Match with iNat Observation to Fetch Data… (right-click a photo in the list, or Your photo; Ctrl- or Shift-click first to pick several photos of the same observation) — when a name won't match, perhaps because iNaturalist has since renamed the species, find your own iNaturalist observation of the photo and take its species and/or coordinates, as in the library. It looks first at observations made within 10 minutes of each photo (iNaturalist usually takes an observation's time from its photo), read from the photos' own EXIF time; Taken within widens it to 2 hours or a day either side, and each choice shows how many it would find. Photos with no time are matched by their date. The dialog shows the name the photo is filed under beside iNaturalist's; taking the species confirms the photo and, as Confirm does, teaches PhotoLife that name for it. Your photos are shown on the left of the dialog (its ← → buttons step through several), beside your observations.

Most of these have keyboard shortcuts — see Keyboard shortcuts — so you can work through a long queue without reaching for the mouse.

Browsing & coverage

The Photos of Tree Selection tab follows your selection in the tree: pick a taxon and you see its cover photo, its details, and a grid of just its photos. The Reassign to Taxon… button moves the selected photos (or, with none selected, every photo shown) to a different taxon. The box under it shows Any location, or only the photos With a location (GEO), or only those Without a location — handy for finding the ones still to geotag with Paste Location or Set Location on Map… (the Map tab follows it too). The Coverage column in the tree shows a ✔ for photographed species, a · for missing ones, and a “have / total” count on genera and families.

The panel under the tree summarises the whole tree: species photographed, threatened-taxa coverage broken out by status (listed subspecies and varieties count as taxa in their own right), introduced species photographed (on the right, with the share of the tree's species they make up), the most recent capture, and how many reference photos are cached. Hover a taxon in the tree to see its status and whether it's introduced. Click the threatened summary, or any one status tier, to filter both the tree and the photos to just those taxa; Clear filter undoes it. While filtered, those species are coloured in the tree: green ✔ for ones you've photographed, red ✗ for ones you haven't. Click the introduced summary on the right to do the same for the tree's introduced species: they're shown in blue with an asterisk in the Coverage column (✔ * if you've photographed them, * if not). The introduced and threatened filters replace each other; click the active one again, or Clear filter, to go back to the whole tree. While one of them is on, the genera and families count only the filtered taxa (the Coverage heading says which, such as Coverage: introduced), so a genus with one introduced species reads 0 / 1 rather than its count of every species. Whenever any filter narrows the tree — these, Only taxa I’ve photographed or the coverage box — a highlighted line above the tree says what's shown, and its Show all clears every filter at once.

The Unphotographed Taxa tab is a plain list of the gaps, with its own filter box — activate an entry to jump to it in the tree, or right-click it to view its reference photo. A line above it counts what's missing: species, and subspecies, varieties, forms and hybrids. Untick Show subspecies, varieties and hybrids to list species only; ticked, a missing subspecies or variety sits under its species, and a hybrid under its genus. The choice is remembered, and the tab's count follows it. Tick Photo grid to see the same taxa as a grid of small iNaturalist reference photos instead of a list: double-click one to find it in the tree, or right-click to view a larger image or open it on iNaturalist. Taxa without a reference photo yet show an empty square; Fetch Reference Photos, on the Reference Photos tab, gets them. The Only taxa I’ve photographed checkbox above the tree collapses it to just the branches you have a photo somewhere in — a quick “what have I got” view. Untick it to see the whole reference tree again.

The Photographic Life List tab lists the tree's species you've photographed in the order you first photographed them, numbered 1, 2, 3… — number 1 is the species with the earliest dated photo of any in the tree. Each entry shows the date of that first photo, its names, where it was taken and how many photos you have of it. A photo of a subspecies or variety counts for its species too, so the earlier of them dates the species. Tick Show subspecies, varieties and hybrids to number those as entries of their own as well. Tick Thumbnails to see each entry's first photo; double-click a row, or right-click it and choose View Full Screen, to see it full size (the arrow keys then step through the list's first photos in order). Right-click also offers Show All Photos of This Taxon and Open on iNaturalist. Click a column heading to sort by it; click # to return to the life list order. Dates come from each photo's EXIF data or its file name; a photo with no date, or one later than today (a misread file name, say), doesn't date an entry, and species with only such photos come last, unnumbered. Both ticks are remembered. Columns… (or a right-click on the column headings) chooses which columns show — including the Camera and Focal length of each entry's first photo, read from its EXIF the first time those columns are shown (in the background, a few seconds for a few hundred photos) and remembered after that. Photos only in the cloud aren't downloaded for it.

The coverage box beside it filters to Fully covered taxa (Coverage X / X, or ✔ for a species) or Not fully covered ones, among the ranks you've chosen to show with Ranks…. For example, show only genera and pick Not fully covered to list the genera you're still missing species from. Greyed rows aren't a match themselves; they're only there as the path to one (such as the incomplete family a complete genus belongs to). It combines with Only taxa I’ve photographed: together with Not fully covered they show taxa you've photographed some, but not all, of.

Tick Seen column to keep track of the species you've seen but haven't photographed yet. Each species, subspecies, variety and hybrid gets a checkbox: the ones you've photographed are ticked already (greyed — the tick comes from the photos), and you tick the others yourself, or right-click one and choose Seen (Not Photographed). Ticking a subspecies counts its species as seen too (untick the subspecies to undo it). Genera, families and the ranks above show how many of their species are seen, as seen / total. Under the tree, a line adds them up — Seen 340 of the 1,204 species shown — 312 photographed, 28 seen only — counting only the taxa the filters leave showing. A tick belongs to the taxon, so it shows in every tree that has it; when you photograph a species later, its tick simply becomes a photographed one.

Photos not in any tree

The Not in Any Tree tab lists taxa your photos are matched to that are missing from your trees — for example a subspecies a tree doesn't list, or a species outside every tree's scope. Its Show box switches between taxa not in any tree, taxa not in the selected tree, and photos identified only to genus or higher. Select taxa to see their photos; right-click one (or use Add to Selected Tree) to add it to a tree.

Reference photos & best shots

The Reference Photos tab shows one iNaturalist photo per species in the current tree, for comparison while you're out identifying things. PhotoLife fetches missing ones automatically the first time you open the tab for a tree with gaps, or on demand via Reference Tree → Fetch Reference Photos.

Right-click a reference photo and choose View Larger Image to see it up close: PhotoLife downloads iNaturalist's largest copy of that photo (up to 2048 pixels; the grid holds only small ones), keeps it for next time, and opens it in the image viewer — where A shows actual pixels and you can zoom and pan — with the photographer's credit underneath. Open on iNaturalist (or a double-click) opens the species' iNaturalist page.

Export Contact Sheet… on the tab (also Reference Tree → Export Reference Photo Contact Sheet (PDF)) saves the photos it currently lists — the whole tree, or just the selected branch — as a printable PDF grid of thumbnails with their names. Pick the page size and how many thumbnails per row (Small, Medium, Large or a custom count), a title and subtitle, whether to show the date, species count and page numbers, and optionally group the photos under Order, Family or Genus headings — each group on a new row, or (with Start each group on a new row off) as one continuous, full grid with a small label above each group’s photos. Names too long for a narrow thumbnail are shrunk or wrapped onto a second line, never cut off. Most iNaturalist photos are Creative Commons licensed, so photo credits are included by default as a numbered list on a final page; they can go under each photo instead, or be left out. Photos not yet downloaded are fetched first.

Right-click any of your own photos (in any grid) to star it as the Best Shot of its species — only available once the photo is identified. Starred photos get a gold star badge on their thumbnail and show up together in the My Best Shots tab. The full-size viewer also has an Add/Remove Best Shot button. A photo already rated 5 stars when it's added to the library — by Organise's B, or in Lightroom, darktable or another program (in the photo or its .xmp sidecar) — arrives as a Best Shot; unstar it here and it stays unstarred.

Nature Photo of the Day

When PhotoLife opens, a photo from your library fills most of the screen: the Nature Photo of the Day. It's the same photo all day, and a new one each day. Your Best Shots come first; once each has had its day, it picks from all your identified photos, and when every one has been shown it starts again. Only identified photos (a confirmed or automatic match, not an unreviewed guess) whose files are on this computer are chosen.

Under the photo are its common and scientific names and its whole lineage, from Life down to the species (hover over a step for its rank and common name), with when and where it was taken. Show in Library finds it in All Library Photos; F11 makes it full screen.

To stop it popping up, untick Show a Nature Photo of the Day when PhotoLife opens. View → Nature Photo of the Day… shows it at any time, and View → Show Photo of the Day at Start-up turns it back on.

Renaming photos on your drive

Select one or more photos in the library, right-click them and choose Rename Files on Drive…. This renames the actual files on your drive, not just how PhotoLife shows them. A photo's RAW, JPEG and editing sidecars (.xmp, .dop…) are renamed together, and the catalogue follows, so matches, best shots and locations stay with the photo. There are two ways to name them:

  • Change the name part: the name at the start of each file name is replaced and the rest kept, so Wasp Gall 1 - Sailors Tk, Steiglitz 14-6-2020 (1).JPG becomes Apiomorpha - Sailors Tk, Steiglitz 14-6-2020 (1).JPG. Beside it, choose how each photo is named from its identified species: Common name, Scientific name, Common name (Scientific name) or Scientific name (Common name) — photos of different species each get their own, and a species without a common name goes by its scientific one. Or type a name (As typed) for them all. Picking a form also fills in the folder's new name the same way, unless you've changed it.
  • Build the whole name from parts: Choose Parts… opens the same builder as exporting, with scientific or common name, date, time, town, Locality (from file name), author, sequence number and your own text. The pattern is remembered for next time, apart from the export's.

When the photos are all in one folder, tick Also rename the folder to rename it too. It's filled in with the species' name, scientific or common to match how the folder is named now, and you can change it. The folder's other photos and the folders inside it go with it. A folder you added in Settings (a library root) isn't renamed here.

The list shows every photo's name now and after before anything changes. Nothing is ever overwritten: a name already taken in the folder gets the next (2), (3). Photos kept online only are left as they are. If you change your mind, right-click and choose Undo Last Rename on Drive to put the names back; it works until PhotoLife closes. Other programs that remember the old names, such as a Lightroom catalogue, won't follow. If you sync your photos online, the renamed files upload under their new names, and the old names stay online until you take out photos deleted here.

The map & photo localities

The Map tab plots every geotagged photo in the current tree selection as a pin on the map, scoped exactly like Photos of Tree Selection. Map and Satellite in its corner switch between OpenStreetMap's street map and Esri's aerial photos (World Imagery); Set Location on Map has the same switch, and PhotoLife remembers your choice for both. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, and use Fit to Photos to frame everything in view — it does this automatically whenever you change the tree selection. The active tree's region is outlined and lightly shaded, using the boundary iNaturalist has for that place (fetched once, in the background); turn this off with View → Show Tree Locality on Map. Restrict to Active Reference Tree Locality hides photos taken outside that boundary. For the few places iNaturalist has no boundary for, the region's bounding rectangle is used instead, which takes in a little of the neighbouring regions. Photos taken close together cluster into one pin showing a count; click a single-photo pin to open it. Clicking a cluster lists the taxa photographed there, each with its number of photos; click a taxon to see its photos, then a photo to open it. ‹ All taxa goes back to the list, and the ✕ button, Esc or a click elsewhere on the map closes it. Only photos whose file carried GPS data appear here — look for the GEO badge on a thumbnail elsewhere in the app, or hover it for the exact coordinates.

Library → Fetch Photo Localities looks up a place name (suburb, park, region) for each geotagged photo from OpenStreetMap. It goes slowly, to respect OpenStreetMap's usage limits, and remembers results, so it only needs doing once per photo. Turn on Locality under View → Display… to show it under thumbnails. When you give photos a location yourself (Set Location on Map…, pasting a location, or from an iNaturalist observation), PhotoLife looks up the new spot's place name straight away, so the Photographic Life List's Where column fills in.

Exporting a photo collection

Reference Tree → Export Photo Collection… copies the current tree's matched photos into a folder structure that mirrors the tree — a tidy copy to share or back up. Your originals stay where they are. You can include automatic matches or just the ones you've confirmed, create folders for taxa you haven't photographed yet, match exactly what the tree is currently showing, and put common names in folder names. Untick Include RAW files to copy only JPEGs and keep the collection small; photos that have only a RAW file are then left out. Or choose Thumbnails only to write a small 256 px JPEG for each photo instead of its original files — made from its JPEG, or from its RAW file when there's no JPEG, and keeping its date taken, camera and GPS location. A collection keeps whichever it was first exported with. A preview shows which photos will be copied.

Naming the exported files

Copies keep their original filenames unless you tick Rename the copied files using a naming pattern. Naming Pattern… opens a tool for building your own naming protocol from parts: scientific name, common name, date taken, time taken, year, town, state, country (from the photo's GPS location), author, the original filename, a sequence number and any fixed text you type. Put the parts in order, choose the separator and date format, and the preview shows the names a few of your own photos would get, for example Caladenia_carnea_2020-01-09_Anglesea_001.jpg.

  • A part a photo has no value for, such as a common name or a location, is left out of that photo's name.
  • A photo's RAW and JPEG get the same name, each with its own extension.
  • Sequence number numbers photos that would otherwise share a name (001, 002, …) in date order. If two photos in a folder still end up with the same name, the second gets “ (2)”.
  • Author is the name you type in the tool. The same name is used for every file.
  • Only the copies are named this way. Your library photos keep their own names.

A collection keeps the naming pattern it was first exported with, so updating it doesn't add second copies under new names. When an update renumbers photos, copies already in the collection are recognised by their content.

Choose Update an existing collection to add new photos to a collection you exported before; PhotoLife checks it's the right folder, skips photos already there, and lists any files in the collection it didn't put there.

Before copying anything, PhotoLife checks the destination can be written to, so a drive that's unplugged or a network or cloud mount that's offline is reported straight away. If the destination stops responding partway through, the export stops and says so. Photos that couldn't be copied are listed, and exporting again later picks up where it left off. Cloud-drive mounts (such as rclone) need to be online to export to; to work offline, export to a local folder and sync that folder to the cloud separately.

iNaturalist tools

The iNaturalist view (toolbar, View menu or iNaturalist Tools menu) holds the features that work with an iNaturalist account rather than a particular reference tree. The account bar at the top is shared by all of them: your iNaturalist username, and optionally a personal API token from inaturalist.org/users/api_token (it expires after about 24 hours). Without a token, geoprivacy-obscured species come back with fuzzed coordinates, even for your own observations. iNaturalist Tools → iNaturalist Account jumps straight to it.

Get Token… saves pasting the token: it opens iNaturalist's token page in your browser (sign in there if asked), and as soon as you press Ctrl+A then Ctrl+C on that page, PhotoLife takes the token from what you copied, saves it and clears it from the clipboard. A small window stays above your browser meanwhile, saying what's happening: that it's watching the clipboard (for five minutes), that something you copied isn't a token, that it has your token and is checking it with iNaturalist, and then whose account it is. You can paste the token into it instead. Beside the token you see until when it's good — “Signed in until 3:40 pm tomorrow”.

Download My Observations finds photos from your own observations that aren't in your library yet.

  • Choose a Tree (it follows the tree selected on the left until you pick one yourself), then search either Species in this tree or All my observations. Restrict to this tree's locality limits either search to the tree's region.
  • Limit to taxon narrows either search to one group (a family, genus, order…) and everything beneath it: pick one with Choose Taxon…, or use the taxon selected in the Reference Trees panel with Use Tree Selection. All Taxa removes the limit.
  • Results are badged NEW (no photos of that taxon in your library) or NO TREE (the taxon isn't in any reference tree); Show lists just those. Faded photos look like ones you already have; Select All skips them.
  • Download Selected… saves full-resolution originals, with EXIF author, GPS, date and taxon, into a new timestamped folder inside the folder you choose. It then adds that folder to your library and matches just those photos. The rest of PhotoLife is locked until it's done; Cancel stops after the current photo and still adds what was downloaded.
  • Photos come from iNaturalist's storage on Amazon S3, which occasionally drops a connection even on a good network, so each photo is retried a few times before being skipped. Any that still fail are listed at the end and left selected, so Download Selected… again retries just those.

Compare Species sets an iNaturalist observer's taxa against your reference trees and photo library — for example, to find species you've recorded on iNaturalist that are missing from a tree.

  • By default the Observer is you, but any username works.
  • Choose a Rank (species, genus, family…); subspecies count as their species, species as their genus, and so on. Choose which Observations count (all, verifiable, or research grade only), what to Compare with (one tree or all of them) and, optionally, a Limit to taxon group.
  • After you press Compare, the counts across the top (On iNat, not in tree, On iNat, not in library, In tree, not on iNat…) filter the table below when clicked. Export CSV… saves the rows shown.
  • Right-click a row to see those observations on iNaturalist, show the taxon in its tree, add it to the tree, or jump to Download My Observations for just that taxon.
  • Taxa observed only above the chosen rank (e.g. a genus-only record when comparing species) can't be placed and are reported separately.

Uploading observations to iNaturalist

Upload Observations, the third tab of the iNaturalist view (also iNaturalist Tools → Upload Observations), sends photos from your library to your iNaturalist account as new observations, each with a species, a date and a location. One observation can have several photos.

  • Adding photos. Add Photos… searches the library by when the photos were taken, their folder and part of a species' scientific or common name. For the folder, type part of its name, or press Browse… to choose one on your drive (with or without its subfolders). Select the photos you want and press Add. Or select photos anywhere in the library, right-click and choose Upload to iNaturalist….
  • New photos. Only photos in the library can be uploaded, so PhotoLife can remember which ones were. If the folder you choose has photos the library doesn't, Add Photos says so and offers Add Folder to Library, as Library → Add Folder to Library does. Its new photos are then matched to species by their names, as Match Library does; photos named only by the camera (DSC_0001.JPG) get no species, so use Identify… for those. Your files aren't moved or changed. Once that's done, the new photos are selected, ready to add.
  • "Only photos not yet on iNaturalist" leaves out photos the catalogue knows are on iNaturalist: those you uploaded from here, those that came from Download My Observations, and those you've said are the same sighting as an observation there (below). It can't know about photos you posted from the iNaturalist app or website; the check before uploading looks for those.
  • Observations. Photos of the same species taken no more than the gap apart (30 minutes to start with) become one observation; Regroup applies a new gap. Photos without a species yet only group with photos taken within 2 minutes of them (a burst), since unsorted photos half an hour apart are often of different things. Once you've given them a species, Regroup joins those of the same species. Tick and within to split by distance too: a photo taken further than that (500 m to start with) from the last one with a location starts a new observation; photos without a location go by time alone. Each observation lists its photos beneath it; Photo size makes them bigger or smaller, and double-clicking a photo shows it full screen (Esc closes it, F11 makes it a window). It takes the species each photo is matched to (a confirmed match first), the date of its earliest photo, and the location of the first photo that has one.
  • Photo order. The photos go in the order listed, and the first, marked Cover, is the one iNaturalist shows. Drag a photo to move it, or use the arrow buttons (Alt+Up, Alt+Down). Dragging a photo onto another observation moves it there, with that observation's species. Photos already sent stay first, in their order.
  • Putting it right. Merge joins the selected observations (asking which species, if they differ); Split Here starts a new observation at the selected photo; Remove takes observations or photos out. Choose Species… picks a species from the catalogue, and Identify… asks iNaturalist's computer vision (iNaturalist is told the species came from its suggestions). Set Location… is Set Location on Map: the location is saved to the photos too. Set Date… is for an observation none of whose photos has a date. Double-click the Note column (or right-click → Note…) to write a description, on as many lines as you like.
  • Copy and paste a location. Right-click a photo with a location → Copy Location, then select photos without one, right-click → Paste Location. It's saved to the photos as Set Location on Map does (below). The same works in the library's photo grids.
  • Geoprivacy applies to everything sent: Open (the location is public), Obscured (the public sees an area of about 20 km, you see the point) or Private.
  • Already on iNaturalist? Before anything is sent, PhotoLife looks for each observation among your own on iNaturalist, and in your library. A match is the same or a related species (a genus or subspecies observation counts, as does one not yet identified), the same day (or within 12 hours when both have a time), and within 1 km (or one of them has no location). Upload always does this first, so you're asked before a likely duplicate is sent; Check iNaturalist looks without uploading. Observations and photos PhotoLife already knows are on iNaturalist (sent from PhotoLife, or downloaded from there) aren't looked for: they show on a light green background, with Sent or On iNaturalist, and the message after a check says how many there are. They're never sent again; an observation with only some of its photos there gets just the missing ones. A photo that's an exact copy of one already on iNaturalist (the same file in another folder) shows as on iNaturalist, and isn't sent.
  • Reviewing a match. An observation that matches one already there is held: unticked, with Maybe already on iNaturalist in its Status. Double-click the Status, or use Review Match…, to see your photos beside that observation's, then choose: Same Sighting — Don't Upload (nothing is sent, and the photos are recorded as of that observation, so they aren't offered again); Add These Photos to It (they're sent to that observation instead of a new one); or Upload as New. Ticking a held observation also uploads it as new.
  • Upload sends the ticked observations that have a species, a date and a location, one at a time; Status says what any other one needs. They're public on iNaturalist as soon as they arrive. Each photo goes as a copy 2048 pixels across, without the file's metadata (a RAW sends its embedded preview); your files aren't changed. Observations that went up whole are then taken off the list (the message underneath links to the last one, to see it on iNaturalist); any that couldn't be sent stay, with the reason in Status, for Upload to try again.

Uploading needs your iNaturalist token (see Get Token… above). It lasts a day: if it runs out partway, the upload stops and says so — get a new token and press Upload again. Every observation and photo iNaturalist accepts is recorded in the catalogue straight away, so nothing is ever sent twice: after a failure, a stop or an expired token, Upload sends only what's missing, adding any photos left over to the observation already made. Photos on iNaturalist show as On iNaturalist when added again, and Download My Observations knows you have them.

Deleted on iNaturalist? If you delete an observation, or a photo from one, on iNaturalist itself, PhotoLife still has it recorded as sent. Right-click those photos (or the observation) on the upload page and choose Not on iNaturalist Any More…. PhotoLife asks iNaturalist what's still there:

  • an observation that's gone is forgotten for all its photos, which then go up as a new observation;
  • a photo taken out of an observation that's still there goes back into it;
  • photos still on iNaturalist are left as they are, unless you choose to send them again anyway.

Then press Upload. If iNaturalist can't be reached, you can still mark the selected photos as not there, and they go up as a new observation.

Identify with iNaturalist

iNaturalist's computer vision — the suggestions its own app makes — can say what a photo shows. PhotoLife sends the photo (a copy 1280 pixels across; a RAW's embedded preview), with where and when it was taken if it knows, to be scored: nothing is posted to iNaturalist and nothing is decided for you. The suggestions come back best first, each with iNaturalist's score, and marked seen nearby (recorded near where it was taken) and in your trees; ones outside your trees are greyed, and Only taxa in my trees hides them. To see what a suggestion looks like, right-click it and choose Show a Photo of It (or press Space on it): iNaturalist's photo of that taxon pops up, with its names and the photographer's credit; a click anywhere, or Esc, closes it. If you'd rather not commit to a species, the same right-click menu lists the ranks above the suggestion — its genus, family, order and so on up to its kingdom — under Use a higher rank instead: choose one and it's used just as a suggestion would be. Where a suggestion names your photos (in Organise), Name with common names chooses between its common name (Flame Robin) and its scientific name (Petroica phoenicea); a taxon without a common name gets its scientific name either way. When the suggestions disagree, the first row gives the group they share, such as Probably within Petroica (genus).

  • Organise: I names the focused photo from a suggestion; Identify Runs… does every unnamed run at once, for you to check.
  • The image viewer: I (or Identify) shows suggestions beside the photo. For a small bird, Shift+drag a box round it first: just that part is sent, from the full-size photo, which often helps a lot. In Organise a suggestion picked names the photo; in the library and Review Unmatched it becomes the photo's match.
  • Review Unmatched: Identify (iNaturalist) puts a picked suggestion at the top of the candidates, ready to confirm.

It needs your iNaturalist token, which lasts a day: when there isn't one (or it has run out) PhotoLife says so and offers Get Token…, then carries on by itself once you've copied the new one. If iNaturalist refuses or is busy, try again a little later. Answers are kept until PhotoLife closes, so asking again about the same photo is instant.

The image viewer

Double-click any thumbnail to open the full-size viewer. Previous/Next (or the Left/Right and Page Up/Down keys) move through the current set, Home/End jump to the ends, and Esc closes it. The star button adds or removes the photo as a best shot. If the photo has GPS data, View on Map opens its location in your browser. I (or Identify) asks iNaturalist what the photo shows — or, after a Shift+drag round part of it, just that part (see Identify with iNaturalist). Opened from Organise, R rejects the photo and N names it (and the photos after it).

Zooming. Press A (or double-click) to switch between fitting the window and actual pixels (1:1, one photo pixel per screen pixel), centred on the spot under the pointer. The mouse wheel zooms in and out around the pointer, +/− step the zoom, and 0 or F fits the photo again; the zoom shows beside the photo count. Zoomed in, the pointer becomes a hand: drag to pan. Moving to the next or previous photo keeps the zoom and the spot, so you can compare a burst's sharpness. (A RAW file without a JPEG shows its embedded preview, which is full size on most cameras.)

Learned names

Matching → Learned Names… lists the corrections PhotoLife has picked up from your reviewing. Forgetting one puts the affected photos back in the queue on the next Match Library run.

What PhotoLife writes to your photos

Everyday use — scanning, matching, reviewing, browsing — only ever reads your photos. PhotoLife writes files only when you ask it to:

  • Download My Observations creates new photo files in the folder you choose.
  • Export Photo Collection writes copies into the folder you choose.
  • Attempt to Match with iNat Observation to Fetch Data (right-click one or more photos) lists your own iNaturalist observations from within a day of the photos — any species, so it works for unmatched photos too. Pick the one that matches by its photo; its species and coordinates are shown, and you tick which to apply to the selected photos. The species is set as a confirmed match. Coordinates are written as Set Location on Map writes them (below), and photos that already have coordinates keep them unless you tick Also replace. With the species you can also tick Also rename the photo files and choose its common or scientific name: the name part of each file name is replaced and the rest kept (Wasp Gall 1 - Sailors Tk 14-6-2020 (1).JPG becomes Apiomorpha - Sailors Tk 14-6-2020 (1).JPG), with the RAW, JPEG and .xmp sidecar renamed together; a name already taken gets the next number, and nothing is overwritten. Photos kept online only aren't renamed. It needs your username in the iNaturalist view.
  • Rename Files on Drive… (right-click photos in the library) renames the photos' files, and their folder if you ask (see Renaming photos on your drive).
  • Set Location on Map… (right-click photos in Organise or the library), and Create Geotag from GPX in Organise, write the location into a JPEG's GPS data, keeping the file's date. A RAW file gets it in an .xmp sidecar beside it — its existing one (Lightroom's DSC_1234.xmp or darktable's DSC_1234.NEF.xmp) or a new DSC_1234.xmp, which moves with the photo when organised; Lightroom, darktable and PhotoLife's own scan read it. The RAW itself isn't changed unless you tick Also write it into the RAW files themselves (remembered): then NEF, CR2, ARW, DNG, ORF, PEF and RW2 files get it too, written into a copy that replaces the original only once it reads back with the same date, camera and the new location (never CR3 or RAF). In the library the catalogue is updated as well, and photos kept online only have it in the catalogue alone.
  • Copy Location and Paste Location (right-click photos in the library or Upload Observations) give photos the location of another: copy it from a photo with one, then paste it onto the others, which get it written as Set Location on Map writes it. It goes on the clipboard as latitude, longitude, so a location copied from Google Maps (right-click a spot, click its coordinates) pastes too. Photos that already have a location keep it unless you say to replace it.

Catalogue, appearance & updates

  • File → Switch Catalogue and File → Manage Catalogues… move between your Local catalogue and any server catalogues (see Local and server catalogues).
  • View → Dark Mode switches between the light and dark themes.
  • The Reference Trees panel on the left is hidden in Review Unmatched, iNaturalist and Organise, which don't need it, so they have the whole width. Press F9 (or View → Reference Trees Panel) to show or hide it; each view remembers your choice.
  • Help → Check for Updates… asks GitHub whether there's a newer release (only when you choose it — PhotoLife never checks on its own). If there is, Go to Downloads opens the download section of the PhotoLife website, which offers the installer for your system, and What's New shows the release notes; installing over your current version keeps your catalogues and settings. If the check can't be done, it says why and still offers the downloads. Help → Storage Usage… shows how much disk space the catalogue and each cache is using. Clear… empties the reference photos or a set of map tiles (they're downloaded again as needed), and Remove Unused… removes the thumbnails of photos that are no longer in any catalogue on this computer. Map tiles are kept under a limit you choose there (500 MB for each set to start with): when a set passes it, the oldest tiles are removed the next time PhotoLife starts. Your photos are never touched by any of this.

Local and server catalogues

PhotoLife keeps completely separate catalogues, each with its own reference trees, photos, folders and review queue:

Version 0.1.20 adds to the catalogue (it remembers the taxa you keep after a refresh), so every computer sharing a server catalogue needs 0.1.20 or later before they can sync with one that has it.

  • Your Local catalogue — your own photo library on your drives. Not shared and never synced.
  • Server catalogues — shared with collaborators through a Postgres server, with the photos in a storage bucket PhotoLife syncs or a folder you share some other way (see Sharing the photos). Each has a working copy on your computer (so PhotoLife stays fast) that syncs with the server only when you ask. You can belong to several.


Left: a Local catalogue. Right: a Server catalogue, where the catalogue and the photos each have a copy online and one on every computer.

The top right of the window always says which catalogue is open — Local catalogue with a LOCAL CATALOGUE badge, or Server: name with the server badge — and so does the window title. Click it (or File → Switch Catalogue) to open another; PhotoLife restarts into it. Switching never copies anything between catalogues. File → Manage Catalogues… lists them all, and adds, edits or removes servers. To bring a reference tree from one catalogue into another, export it (Reference Tree → Export Reference Tree…) and import it there.

Working in a server catalogue

One person edits a server catalogue at a time — there is never anything to merge.

  • Setting up: File → Manage Catalogues… → Add Server…: the server's details, your name (shown to the others while you edit) and this catalogue's photo library folder — where its photos are on your computer. Photo locations are shared relative to that folder, so each person can keep it wherever suits them, even on a different operating system. The first time you open it, PhotoLife downloads the server's catalogue — or, for an empty server, starts it from a copy of your Local catalogue or empty. Your Local catalogue itself is never changed.
  • What mode am I in? The badge at the top right of the window always says: grey VIEWING, blue NEWER VERSION AVAILABLE, amber VIEWING — Alex is editing, green EDITING · 12 changes, red OFFLINE, or SYNCING…. Beside it are the buttons that make sense right now, and the window title says Editing or Viewing. Click the badge for Server Status: the connection, who holds the edit lock and since when, your version and the server's (and who last updated it), your unsent changes, your photo library folder and your backups.
  • Viewing: you can browse, filter, view photos and export; anything that would change the catalogue offers to Start Editing first. Get Latest downloads a newer catalogue when there is one (PhotoLife restarts to open it).
  • Editing: Start Editing takes the edit lock (getting the latest catalogue first if there's a newer one); while you hold it, everyone else can only view. Changes (Server → Review Changes) lists everything you've changed since your last sync, by kind — photo identifications, trees, best shots, learned names… — each expandable to the individual items; automatic updates such as cover photos are grouped separately. Sync sends your changes and keeps the lock; Finish Editing sends them and lets others edit. Both first show a summary of what will be sent. Closing PhotoLife while editing asks whether to finish.
  • Server menu: everything above, plus Discard My Changes… (back to your last sync, optionally stopping editing), Activity Log… (every server action on this computer, with times, results and any problems), Test Connection (how long connecting and a round trip take), Take Over Editing… (for when someone forgot to finish — their unsent changes can then no longer be sent), Restore a Backup… and Switch to Local Catalogue….
  • Backups: whenever a catalogue is replaced — by Get Latest, Discard or Restore — the one it replaces is kept beside it (the last three), and can be brought back with Restore a Backup. What a restored backup differs by shows as your changes; nothing is sent until you Sync while editing.
  • Photos outside your library folder can't be opened by the others; a sync that sends any says how many.

Sharing the photos

The server holds the catalogue, not the photos. Each person needs the photos in their own photo library folder, and there are two ways to get them there (Server → Server Settings… → Photo storage):

  • Shared some other way — you keep the folder in step yourself, for example with a cloud-drive app. PhotoLife just reads the folder.
  • PhotoLife syncs them — the photos are kept in an S3-compatible storage bucket (Cloudflare R2, Backblaze B2, Amazon S3…), and PhotoLife keeps a full copy in your photo library folder. Scans and thumbnails work at local-disk speed, and everything works offline. Enter the bucket's endpoint, region, bucket name and an access key, then Test Photo Storage. Everyone uses the same bucket (and folder in it, if any), each with their own key if you like.
  • PhotoLife syncs them — a shared Google Drive folder — the same, but the photos are kept in a Google Drive folder shared with everyone, as ordinary folders and files you can also see on the Drive website (under library, with thumbnails under thumbs). No mounting or Drive app is needed.

Setting up Google Drive (once, by one person):

  1. In Google Cloud Console, create a project, enable the Google Drive API, and set up the OAuth consent screen. Add each collaborator's Google account as a test user — or, if you're all in one Google Workspace, make it Internal.
  2. Under Credentials, create an OAuth client ID of type Desktop app, and share its client ID and secret with the group. For a desktop app the secret isn't really secret; sign-ins are protected another way (PKCE).
  3. Create the shared folder in Drive and share it (as Editor) with everyone.

Then each person, in Server Settings → Photo storage, enters the client ID and secret, pastes the folder's link, clicks Sign In with Google… and approves in the browser, then Test Photo Storage. Until Google reviews the OAuth client, it works only for the listed test users, and each sign-in lasts 7 days — when it runs out, PhotoLife says so and you sign in again (an Internal Workspace client doesn't have this limit). For a large library, an S3-compatible bucket is simpler to run.

When PhotoLife syncs the photos:

  • Sync and Finish Editing upload your new and changed photos before sending the catalogue, so the others can always get every photo it refers to. If some can't be uploaded, PhotoLife asks before sending the catalogue anyway.
  • New photos from the others download in the background when PhotoLife starts and after Get Latest. Server → Sync Photos does both directions at any time. The status bar and Server Status show how many are waiting.
  • Photos are compared by content, so a first sync of a folder that's already in both places (say, one you used to share with a cloud drive) transfers nothing that already matches.
  • A photo changed both here and online is kept twice: yours under its name and theirs beside it as name (from Alex).jpg.
  • A photo deleted online is moved from your folder to .photolife-trash inside it, by date, rather than deleted.
  • A photo you delete here isn't downloaded again unless someone changes it. Whether it also comes out of the photo storage is set by Photos deleted here in Server Settings: Ask me after each sync (the default) lists them after an upload and offers Remove Online, Keep Online or Decide Later; Remove them online too does it during each upload; Keep them online never does. Server → Remove Deleted Photos Online… reviews them at any time, including ones kept online before.
  • Removing never deletes outright: on Google Drive the photos go to its bin (kept for 30 days); in S3 storage they move under trash/ in the bucket. Collaborators' copies then move to their .photolife-trash at their next sync. Only photos that were synced here and are unchanged online since are ever removed, never copies freed up for space or photos never downloaded. If so many photos vanish at once that it looks like a disconnected drive or a moved folder, nothing is removed, and a library folder that's suddenly empty stops the sync altogether.
  • Downloads are checked against the bucket's checksum before they replace anything, and an interrupted sync carries on where it stopped next time.
  • You can stop a photo sync at any time: Server → Stop Photo Sync, the Stop Photo Sync button in Server Status, or Cancel in the upload window before a Sync. Every photo already transferred stays done; the next sync (Sync Photos, or your next Sync) carries on with the rest. A photo stopped half-way is sent again from its start. If you stop the upload before a Sync, the catalogue isn't sent either, so it never refers to photos the others can't get.
  • Only photos (JPEG, PNG, TIFF and RAW files) are synced; sidecar and other files aren't.

Keeping only some photos on this computer

A big shared library doesn't have to fit on every computer. Server → Photos on This Computer… keeps either every photo, or only the photos of the reference trees you tick — each listed with how many photos it has and how much space they take. Everyone chooses for themselves; it changes nothing for the others.

  • A photo belongs to a tree when it's matched (automatically or confirmed) to a taxon in that tree, so a photo can be in several trees. It's kept if it's in any tree you tick.
  • Photos still waiting for review, photos matched to taxa that aren't in any tree, and photos you've downloaded by opening them are always kept. A RAW and its JPEG always go together.
  • The rest stay online only: they still appear everywhere with their thumbnails and an ONLINE badge. Opening one offers to download it, right-click → Download to This Computer fetches a selection, and exporting a tree offers to download its online-only photos first. (Small photos, up to 64 KB, are about the size of a thumbnail, so none is kept online for them: PhotoLife fetches the photo itself to make one.)
  • After you untick trees, PhotoLife offers to free up space by moving the copies here of photos you no longer keep into .photolife-trash in the library folder. Only copies that are unchanged and safely online are moved; tick the tree again and they download again. PhotoLife never deletes photos on your computer, so the space is freed once you empty .photolife-trash yourself.
  • Your own new photos are always uploaded, whichever trees you keep, and Library → Remove Photos With Missing Files… never counts online-only photos as missing.

Cloudflare R2 has no charge for downloads, so it suits a library several people download in full. The access key and secret are stored in your OS settings, not encrypted, like the catalogue password.

Where PhotoLife keeps things

The catalogue database, thumbnail cache, reference-photo cache and map-tile cache live in your user data folder (~/.local/share/PhotoLife on Linux, %LOCALAPPDATA%\PhotoLife on Windows). Deleting that folder resets PhotoLife without affecting a single photo in your library.

RAW files

When a shot has both a RAW and a JPEG, PhotoLife shows the JPEG. For a RAW with no JPEG sibling it reads the embedded preview — if this build was compiled with LibRaw. Otherwise a RAW-only capture shows a plain placeholder tile; the photo is still fully catalogued and matched.

If some of your RAW photos show up in the wrong place on the map, run Library → Fix RAW Photo Locations… once — it corrects a since-fixed bug where a RAW file's GPS hemisphere (north/south, east/west) could be read incorrectly. It only re-reads location data; nothing else about your catalogue changes.

Command line

For scripting and first-time bulk imports, the same steps are available headless:

PhotoLife --scan <folder>
PhotoLife --build-project "<name>" --taxon <taxon> --rank <rank> --place "<region>"
PhotoLife --import-checklist <file.csv> --into "<project>" --source "<label>"
PhotoLife --import-lightroom <catalog.lrcat> --into "<project>"
PhotoLife --match

--match also prints any photos you identified that have since been renamed to a different taxon; settle those in the app with the next Match Library.
